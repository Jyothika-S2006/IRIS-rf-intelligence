# Real-World RF Interference Sources — Knowledge Base

## Common Interference Sources by Frequency Band

### 2.4 GHz Band (IEEE 802.11 b/g/n WiFi, Bluetooth, Zigbee)

| Source | Type | Impact |
|--------|------|--------|
| Microwave oven | Pulsed, ~2.45 GHz | Severe; duty-cycled magnetron desensitises WiFi receivers |
| Baby monitor (analog) | Narrowband FM | Continuous tone; wipes out channel 6 |
| Bluetooth devices | Frequency-hopping | Wideband; FHSS hops every 625 µs |
| Cordless phones (DECT 6.0) | Wideband hopping | Adjacent-channel leakage at 1.9 GHz edge |
| Zigbee / 802.15.4 | Narrowband | Coexists but degrades when WiFi load is high |
| Neighbouring WiFi APs | Adjacent-channel | Dominant source in dense urban deployments |

### 5 GHz Band (IEEE 802.11 a/n/ac/ax WiFi)

| Source | Type | Impact |
|--------|------|--------|
| Weather radar (5.3–5.7 GHz) | Pulsed chirp | Triggers DFS; AP must vacate channel |
| Military radar | Pulsed, wide BW | DFS event; high PAPR, wide occupied BW |
| Outdoor WiFi bridges | Adjacent-channel | High-power sidelobe leakage onto indoor APs |
| Unlicensed 5 GHz devices | Wideband | Raises noise floor across multiple channels |

### Cellular / LTE / 5G NR

| Source | Type | Impact |
|--------|------|--------|
| Passive intermodulation (PIM) | Wideband noise | Generated by corroded connectors in the base-station antenna path |
| Rogue femtocell | Adjacent-channel | Uncoordinated handset association |
| GPS jammer | Narrowband at 1.575 GHz | Illegal; disrupts timing synchronisation |
| LTE uplink noise | Wideband | Uplink power control failure floods the band |

### Satellite / GNSS

| Source | Type | Impact |
|--------|------|--------|
| L-band satellite phone (Iridium, Inmarsat) | Narrowband | CW tone near GPS L1 (1.575 GHz) |
| Broadband LEO uplink (Starlink, OneWeb) | Wideband | Wideband noise in Ku/Ka band |
| TV uplink earth station sidelobe | CW + adjacent | Adjacent-channel leakage on co-located dishes |

## Interference Identification Heuristics

1. **Spectral spike (CW):** Single bin dominates PSD → narrowband source (oscillator, jammer).
2. **Flat noise floor rise:** Spectral flatness near 1 → broadband source (switching PSU, GPU, welder).
3. **Asymmetric shoulder:** Band power ratio ≠ 1, skewed PSD → adjacent transmitter.
4. **Horizontal STFT stripes:** Power bursts at fixed interval → pulsed source (radar, microwave oven).
5. **Random high-power spikes:** Very high spectral kurtosis → impulsive noise (motor brush, ESD).

## Regulatory Context

- FCC Part 15 (USA) / ETSI EN 301 893 (EU): unlicensed devices must not cause harmful interference
  and must accept interference from licensed services.
- Dynamic Frequency Selection (DFS): 5 GHz APs must detect radar and vacate the channel within 10 s.
- Emission masks: transmitters must meet spectral mask requirements limiting sidelobe power.
- Identifying an intentional jammer should be reported to the national spectrum regulator
  (FCC Enforcement Bureau, Ofcom, TRAI, etc.).
